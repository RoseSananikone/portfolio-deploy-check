I attended a product design meet up conference on the 27th of October 2026. They had 4 speakers: Polestar, Nothing, Moonbird and Miro. They each had interesting things to say with different perspective on things. All of these talks were more focused on UI/UX and their own journeys throug a company or how a work process looked like.

### Polestar
Aloka Muddukrishna went through the different steps of designing in terms of UI and UX for their cars at Polestar. He went through the different fonts and colours that they strictly use to keep consistency and for their branding, with a minimalist scandinavian approach too. He also shared the challenges that they have to think about such as making display screens that are not too distracting for drivers but accessible, making buttons big enough and which ones should be closer to the driver. There were a lot of different small things that they had to think about and he also mentioned how cars are slowly bringing back more tactile and physical knobs too since that was a feature that people were starting to miss and complain about the lack of.

### Nothing
Melanie Daveid shared her experience and journey of her job as a whole. How she went from a graphic designer and more visual background to now a senior UX/UI role. She shared her worries and excitements that she had along the way, how the change of directon allowed her to explore more things and also how every now and then, she still stays in touch with her visual background and experimenting with new tools that are always evolving like AI. She mentioned a few times about just starting something and trying things out without worrying too much about the final product and somehow that woke me up a bit because I have felt a bit blocked with creating things and trying new things out so it almost felt like it burst my bubble, which was very good.

### Moonbird
From moonbird, it was an alumni from Devine, Yordi Uytersprot, who was giving the talk which I was not expecting so it was a fun suprise. He talked about the process and research that they did for their product but also shared what went wrong and what they would have done differently. It felt very honest and like a gentle reminder to not always have to come up with new things (like their charger) and sticking to what is common is also alright, especially for cables or inputs/outputs so everyone can use it, like using a usb charging cable.

### Miro
This one was a bit harder to follow because I am not familiar with Miro and the speaker, Warwick Kay, was mostly focusing on features on Miro and the AI agent on it.
