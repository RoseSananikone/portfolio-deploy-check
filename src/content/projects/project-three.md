Bokeh Identity is a "not giving a f*ck" workshop about one question: *"Where are you really from?"* It's made for culturally confused chameleons, people of mixed heritage who have never lived in their "home" country and freeze up every time someone asks.

The booking form is the start of the workshop itself. Instead of a standard order form, it's a calm, soft-focus experience that gently tells you it's okay not to have one simple answer.

## The Task

We were given wireframes for two fictional workshops and had to choose one: "Not Giving a F*ck" or "Fermentation and Preservation". The assignment was to create a concept, a name, a logo and a tone of voice, and to design a creative booking form where ordering is already part of the workshop experience. The form had to include all the questions from the wireframes, give feedback at every step, and end with a confirmation screen personalized to the user's choices.

The result is a clickable prototype in Figma, designed for a MacBook Air (1280 × 832 px).

## Where It Started

When I moved and had to introduce myself at university, I suddenly didn't know which country to name. France, Germany, Vietnam or Laos, all of them were true, but none of them sounded quite right. Every time I answered, it felt a bit like impostor syndrome.

I realized I'm not the only one, so I designed the workshop I would have wanted: one that helps you stop worrying about giving the "right" answer.

**Target audience:** Culturally confused chameleons, adults (18+) of mixed heritage who have never lived in their "home" country and bug out whenever someone asks where they're from.

## The Concept

The name comes from **bokeh**, the soft, out-of-focus blur in photography. Edges are soft, but the shapes are still clearly there. That's the whole idea: your identity can be blended *and* clear at the same time.

The visual language follows this:
- **Blur with soft edges** to create a calm atmosphere and show that blurred lines are still lines.
- **Multicolored watercolor flowers** whose colors mix and mingle, as a symbol of mixed identity.
